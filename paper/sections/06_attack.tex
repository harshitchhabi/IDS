\section{The Attack}
\label{sec:attack}

\subsection{S0: the honest loop works}
% DECISIONS.md s17 point 5: synthetic, fixed threshold, ratio 0.2, within 1-2 rounds.
% honeypot_only: 0.39 (control) -> 0.997 (RF), 0.34 -> 0.994 (XGBoost). seen_both: 0.946->0.961
% (RF), 0.950->0.963 (XGB), above the control band. seed_only flat at ~0.997 (A4's arm,
% untouched as intended). Cost: fixed FPR at 50% rises to 0.024, the class-prior-shift effect
% present with no attacker at all.
Before any attack means anything, the clean loop has to improve the detector. On synthetic
data, at poison ratio 0.2, it does, within one to two rounds: \texttt{honeypot\_only} TPR — the
arm the detector saw only through the decoy — rises from 0.39 (control) to 0.997 for RF and
0.34 to 0.994 for XGBoost. \texttt{seen\_both} rises more modestly (0.946$\to$0.961 RF,
0.950$\to$0.963 XGBoost) and \texttt{seed\_only} stays flat near its ceiling, untouched, as
A4's controlled arm should be. This has a small cost even with no attacker present: fixed
threshold FPR at 50\% honeypot share rises to 0.024, the class-prior-shift cost of retraining
on more positives.

\subsection{A1: a cliff at copy-level fidelity}
% DECISIONS.md s17, s26 (the tau=0.1 re-run): the FPR channel needs realized mimicry distance
% <~0.011-0.014 (raw benign fidelity to the first jitter step) AND >=10-20% poison; damage is
% catastrophic at jitter 0 (RF +0.337/+0.880 at ratio 0.2/0.5) and inside 2 sigma_control
% (0.009 RF, 0.012 XGB) at every jitter >=0.01 tested, for every ratio tested. This SUPERSEDES
% s17's old-calibration table (e.g. +0.468 at 5%) per s19.1/s23; do not use those numbers.
A1's damage on the FPR channel appears at exactly one place: copy-level mimicry fidelity, and
only once enough of the training set is honeypot-derived. At raw benign fidelity (jitter 0,
realized nearest-neighbour distance to trusted benign $\approx0.011$) fixed-threshold FPR rises
catastrophically with poison ratio — RF +0.009/+0.025/+0.337/+0.880 and XGBoost
+0.005/+0.014/+0.279/+0.563 at ratios 0.05/0.1/0.2/0.5 — but at every jitter setting we tested
beyond that (realized distance $\ge0.014$), the same ratios sit inside $2\sigma$ of the
control's own noise for both models, at every ratio tested (Figure~\ref{fig:f3-damage-vs-fidelity}).
There is no gradient to read a distance threshold from: damage does not fall off gradually past
the guard-(c) leakage line (0.25); it is already gone eighteen to twenty-five times closer to
raw benign than that. \emph{These numbers supersede an earlier, miscalibrated pass through this
same sweep (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}); a threshold calibrated on a validation split full of
near-copies of training rows read this cliff as a gentler, flatter effect than it is.}
% DECISIONS.md s26.2/26.3: median distance cannot resolve where the cliff sits (it moves ~1%
% across the jitter range where the damage collapses). Twin fraction (delta=0.0015, from the
% s26.2 p5 gap) does: 0.1053 at jitter 0, 0.00345 at jitter 0.002, 0.0 at every jitter >=0.005.
% Exact-duplicate count between the honeypot pool and trusted_eval, at any jitter, is 0.
The median realized distance cannot actually resolve where this cliff sits: it moves by about
1\% (0.0107 to 0.0108) across the exact jitter step, 0 to 0.002, where the damage collapses from
catastrophic to inside the noise floor. \emph{Twin fraction} — the share of a poison batch's
rows whose nearest trusted-benign neighbour is within RMS distance 0.0015, a delta fixed from
where the fifth-percentile of the realized-distance distribution jumps between those two
jitters (Section~\ref{sec:threat-model}) — does resolve it: 10.5\% of the batch at jitter 0,
0.35\% at jitter 0.002, exactly 0.0 at every jitter $\ge0.005$ tested. This tracks the FPR
collapse point for point and is the x-axis Figure~\ref{fig:f3-damage-vs-fidelity} now plots.
None of these near-twins is a literal duplicate: checking the same pool against trusted\_eval for
exact float32 row matches, at every jitter including 0, finds zero. The jitter-0 damage is
therefore driven by rows within 0.0015 RMS distance of a real benign row, not by byte-identical
copies of one.
% DECISIONS.md s5, s26: raw A1 realized distance at jitter 0 (~0.011) vs. CICIDS benign's own
% median self-distance at the same grid (0.0065, Table 2, Section 5) -- a factor of ~1.7. Stated
% here and returned to in Section 8 (low-entropy networks).
Put plainly: on CICIDS2017, the false-positive channel fires only once the poison sits about as
close to real benign traffic as CICIDS2017's own benign traffic already sits to itself — raw
copy-level poison lands at realized distance $\approx0.011$, within a factor of $\sim1.7$ of
benign's own median self-distance of 0.0065 (Section~\ref{sec:degeneracy}). The attacker does not
need to exfiltrate a specific victim's traffic; replaying the pool's own benign feature
statistics with no perturbation already puts one row in ten inside twin-fraction range, because
CICIDS2017's benign class is itself narrow and repetitive. This channel is not a property of A1
alone; it is a property of how repetitive the target's benign traffic already is, and
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} returns to what that implies for where this attack does and does
not generalize.

\subsection{One mechanism, surfaced by the threshold policy}
% DECISIONS.md s23, s26.1, s26.5 (erratum): one mechanism (label conflict: benign-populated rows
% stamped malicious), one requirement (twin-fraction fidelity). "Two channels" is retired --
% the threshold policy decides which metric shows the damage, not whether it occurs.
% s26.1's Holm-Bonferroni correction across all 36 (model x ratio x jitter) cells for delta_tpr
% (family-wise alpha=0.05): 3 of 36 survive, all at jitter 0 (raw copy fidelity) -- rf ratio 0.2
% (t=-20.3), rf ratio 0.5 (t=-16.6), xgboost ratio 0.5 (t=-8.5). XGBoost ratio 0.2 at jitter 0
% does NOT survive (t=-2.58, p=0.061), so this is "XGBoost at one of two ratios", not "RF-only".
% Nothing at realized distance >=0.25 survives at either ratio for either model. The apparent
% recovery at large jitter in RF's raw means is an observed, unconfirmed pattern -- no mechanism
% proposed.
The same underlying mechanism — rows that sit in benign-populated feature space, stamped
malicious by the auto-labeller — produces all of this section's damage. What differs is not the
mechanism but which metric the threshold policy exposes it through: a fixed threshold reads the
damage as false positives (Section~\ref{sec:attack}, above); a recalibrated threshold reads the
same underlying rows as lost detection, TPR. These were described as two separate channels in an
earlier pass through this data; recalibrating does not remove the damage, it relocates which
number shows it, so we no longer describe them as independent. Both surface at copy-level
mimicry fidelity, once the statistics are held to the standard the rest of this paper uses.
Naively reading off which of the 36 (model, ratio, jitter) cells clear an uncorrected $p<0.05$
suggested TPR damage confined to RF and present at a distance as well as at copy fidelity; that
reading does not survive correcting for the 36 simultaneous comparisons made across the grid.
Applying a Holm-Bonferroni step-down correction (family-wise $\alpha=0.05$) to all 36 cells,
only three survive, and all three are at raw copy fidelity (jitter 0): RF at ratio 0.2
($t=-20.3$) and ratio 0.5 ($t=-16.6$), and XGBoost at ratio 0.5 ($t=-8.5$) — XGBoost's own
ratio-0.2, jitter-0 cell does not clear even its \emph{uncorrected} threshold ($t=-2.6$,
$p=0.06$). Nothing at realized distance $\ge0.25$ survives, at either ratio, for either model
(Table~\ref{tab:two-channels}). \textbf{"Present at every distance tested," "significant near and
significant far," and "the TPR effect is RF-only" — all stated in an earlier reading of this
data — are withdrawn.} The corrected statement is: TPR loss at copy-level fidelity is confirmed
for RF at both poison ratios tested and for XGBoost at one of two. RF's raw means do show damage
recovering at large jitter (Figure~\ref{fig:f3-damage-vs-fidelity}, right column;
Figure~\ref{fig:f4-trajectory}), and it reproduces at both ratios, but 36 simultaneous
comparisons is enough multiplicity that this correction cannot separate it from chance. We
report it as an observed, unconfirmed pattern and propose no mechanism for it. XGBoost shows no
comparable damaging effect anywhere else on the grid; several of its mid-range points are
individually significant before correction, but in the \emph{improving} direction, and none of
them survives correction either.

\textbf{The FPR channel's XGBoost trajectory is seed-dependent, and 5 seeds is too few to
characterize it.} At ratio 0.5, raw fidelity, fixed threshold (Table~\ref{tab:xgb-seeds}),
XGBoost's per-seed FPR at round 10 splits into two groups rather than clustering around the
mean: two seeds have already reached 82--91\%, two sit at 0.6--0.7\%, and one is midway. RF
climbs on every one of the 5 seeds. A mean-over-seeds table hides this — it reads as one noisy
estimate of a common trajectory, and it is not. Every mean XGBoost number elsewhere in this
paper should be read with that in mind; the full 8-round table is in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix}.

\subsection{Mechanism isolation}
% DECISIONS.md s23 "Prior shift is null" table + s7 (this paper's Figure 5 / F5). RF,
% recalibrated threshold, ratio 0.2, realized NN ~0.56, 5 seeds. A1 -0.109+/-0.038 (t=-6.3);
% a1truth +0.014+/-0.029 (n.s.); s0j -0.002+/-0.029 (n.s.); junk +0.015+/-0.033 (n.s., separate
% jitter-1.5 run).
Three matched null controls (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}) isolate which part of A1 is doing
the work. \texttt{a1truth} — A1's own rows, correctly labelled — removes the label conflict and
the effect vanishes ($+0.014\pm0.029$, not significant). \texttt{s0j} — attack rows carrying
A1's jitter, stamped malicious — keeps the volume and the class-prior shift but not the
conflict, and it too is null ($-0.002\pm0.029$). \texttt{junk} — marginal-shuffled bulk, stamped
malicious — tests volume alone and is also null ($+0.015\pm0.033$). Only A1 itself, which keeps
the label conflict, clears the control's own $\pm2\sigma$ band ($-0.109\pm0.038$,
Figure~\ref{fig:f5-mechanism}). Prior shift and raw volume are not the mechanism; the label
conflict is.
% DECISIONS.md s26.1, last paragraph: this experiment (4 arms, one fixed jitter=0.7, ratio 0.2)
% is a separate, small hypothesis family (4 comparisons) from the 36-cell grid sweep above; a1's
% t=-6.3 clears even a Bonferroni threshold within that family of 4 (0.0125). It happens to use
% the same (rf, ratio 0.2, jitter 0.7) cell that does not survive the 36-test correction in
% Section 6.3 -- not a contradiction, but worth stating so the two do not read as inconsistent.
This test was run at one pre-specified jitter (0.7), not swept across the grid in
Section~\ref{sec:attack}'s correction; as a family of four comparisons it needs no correction
beyond what it already clears, even judged by the stricter of the two standards used in this
paper. Section~\ref{sec:attack}'s broader grid sweep, judged against the much larger family of
36 simultaneous tests it runs, could not confirm a damaging effect at this same distance on its
own — the two results do not disagree; they answer different questions (whether the conflict
matters at one pre-registered distance, versus whether damage at that distance is confirmed
against the larger family the grid sweep tests) held to the correction each question's own
family of tests requires. Five seeds is low statistical power for either family. The honest
reading is that this test \textbf{found an effect at one pre-specified distance that the grid
sweep has not yet confirmed generalises} — unconfirmed, not absent.
