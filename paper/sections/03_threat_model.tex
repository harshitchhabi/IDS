\section{Threat Model}
\label{sec:threat-model}
% CLAUDE.md threat model: honeypot-ML integrations retrain the detector on decoy traffic,
% auto-labelled malicious by policy ("everything the honeypot sees is malicious", public), as
% free ground truth. The unexamined assumption is that the label is trustworthy because the
% source is a decoy; the attacker controls exactly what the decoy sees, so the honeypot is a
% write channel into the defender's training set that the defender opens deliberately. The
% poison budget is not a fraction the attacker must fight for -- it is the entire
% honeypot-derived share of the training set.
The systems we attack retrain on two sources: a trusted seed set the defender controls, and
whatever traffic reaches a honeypot, auto-labelled malicious by a public policy — anything a
decoy sees is unsolicited by definition, so the argument goes, and therefore free
ground-truth-malicious data. That argument holds only if the label is trustworthy because the
source is a decoy. It is not: the attacker chooses exactly what the decoy sees, and the
labelling policy is public. The honeypot is a write channel into the defender's training set,
opened by the defender's own design. The poison budget is not a fraction the attacker has to
fight for against a defended channel; it is the entire honeypot-derived share of the training
set, by construction.

% DECISIONS.md s4: attacker capability. Can send arbitrary traffic to the decoy; the pool is
% two-class (benign-shaped and attack-shaped) so A1 (point the benign generator at the decoy)
% and S0/A4 can both be expressed from the same adversary interface; sized (~20k rows) so
% poison ratios up to 50% are reachable with FRESH rows, not resampled duplicates -- a
% resampled duplicate is closer to upweighting an existing sample than to injecting new poison.
\textbf{Attacker capability.} The attacker can send arbitrary traffic to the honeypot and knows
the public labelling policy, but cannot touch \texttt{seed\_train}, \texttt{trusted\_eval}, or
the model directly — every effect it has on the deployed detector routes through what the
honeypot logs. The pool it draws from is two-class, sized so poison ratios up to 50\% of the
training set are reachable with fresh rows rather than resampled duplicates; for a tree model, a
resampled duplicate is closer to upweighting a sample already present than to injecting new
poison, which would understate what the attack can do.

% CLAUDE.md scenarios table.
\textbf{Attacker goal.} Raise the detector's false-positive rate on production traffic, or
suppress its detection of a real attack. \textbf{A1 (benign mimicry)}, this paper's subject,
does the first: point the same kind of traffic generator that produces legitimate benign flows
at the honeypot instead of production, and let the auto-labeller stamp the result malicious.
Nothing about A1 requires the attacker to know anything about the model; it needs only to know
the labelling policy, which is public by the design being attacked. \textbf{S0 (clean)} — the
honeypot sees genuine attacks, correctly labelled — is not an attack; it is the sanity check
this design has to pass before any attack against it means anything (Section~\ref{sec:attack}).
\textbf{A2 (boundary drift)} and \textbf{A4 (fingerprint-and-split)} are scoped out: A2 needs a
gradual multi-round adversary this paper does not build, and A4's controlled measurement arm
cannot occur naturally on CICIDS2017, where every family runs on a single day
(Section~\ref{sec:methodology}).

% DECISIONS.md s18 "Realized fidelity, not the knob": the capability axis is a measured
% distance, not an assumed one.
\textbf{Capability axis.} A1's only free parameter is how closely the attacker's traffic
resembles genuine production benign traffic. We do not assume this from a generation setting;
we measure it directly as the realized nearest-neighbour distance from the injected poison to
trusted benign traffic (Section~\ref{sec:methodology}), and report damage as a function of that
measured distance rather than of the knob that produced it.
